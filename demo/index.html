City of Melbourne · CX concept exploration

CX Journey Intelligence — introducing customer segments and modes into Databricks Genie

I think there's a significant opportunity here, particularly because we're already doing much of the foundational work through the Service Catalogue, Salesforce service mapping, Channel Strategy Y2 and Portal Self-Service Experience analytics.

The opportunity isn't simply to make Genie understand customer segments. It's to turn operational data into evidence of what customers are trying to achieve, where their journeys break down, and where the City of Melbourne should intervene.

That is a different proposition from conventional reporting.

4. A contained three-week test

Week 1 · Connect evidence

Validate parking permit history, support contacts, service relationships, linkage and baseline measures.

Week 2 · Add customer lenses

Configure one governed Genie capability with approved Modes, Segment definitions and benchmark questions.

Week 3 · Validate value

Compare with conventional reporting, review hypotheses with SMEs and record viable opportunities.

Success test: Does the customer lens produce at least one credible, more actionable journey opportunity than operational reporting alone, with traceable evidence and a practical next validation step?

5. What this would — and wouldn't — mean

What Genie could do

Query governed data, compare signals, explore alternative explanations and draft evidence-backed hypotheses for review.

What Genie must not assume

That a permit type reveals a customer's Segment, that two records belong to one journey without validated linkage, or that correlation establishes cause.

The output feeds the existing Measurement Opportunity Register, not an autonomous decision process. The recommended starting point is a bounded test within CX Service Experience Mapping, rather than a separate architecture or multi-agent programme.
